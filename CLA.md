# Contributor License Agreement (CLA)

Omnipus accepts external contributions under a **Contributor License Agreement (CLA)**. Before your first pull request can be merged, you (or your employer, if you contribute on company time) sign the agreement once. After that you're covered for all future contributions to this repository.

## Why we have a CLA

Omnipus is developed by **elicify.ai Pte. Ltd.** as an open-source project under the MIT license. Long-term, the project may also ship as a polished Electron desktop variant and a hosted Cloud / SaaS variant (see [`ROADMAP.md`](ROADMAP.md)). To make those variants possible without retroactively chasing every contributor for permission, we ask each contributor to assign copyright in their contribution to elicify.ai Pte. Ltd.

**The MIT license on the upstream code is unchanged and irrevocable.** Anyone who downloads Omnipus today gets the same MIT terms forever. The CLA only governs *new* contributions from outside contributors. It is not a relicensing of existing code.

## What the CLA does

By signing, you confirm:

1. **You own your contribution** — either you wrote it yourself on your own time, or your employer has authorised you to contribute it.
2. **You assign copyright to elicify.ai Pte. Ltd.** in the contribution, while retaining the right to use your own contribution for any purpose (including in your own derivative works).
3. **You grant a patent license** — if your contribution includes patentable ideas, you give every user of Omnipus a license to use those patents.
4. **You make a series of standard warranties** — that you have the right to contribute, that your contribution is not subject to other agreements that conflict, and so on.

The text is based on the **Apache Individual Contributor License Agreement (ICLA)**, which is the most widely-recognised open-source CLA in the world. It's used by Apache Software Foundation projects, Google, and many others. See: <https://www.apache.org/licenses/icla.pdf>.

A **Corporate CLA (CCLA)** is also available for contributions made on behalf of a company. See: <https://www.apache.org/licenses/cla-corporate.txt>.

## How to sign

> **Status: scaffolding only.** The actual CLA text is being reviewed by counsel; the `cla-assistant.io` integration is being wired up. Outside contributions are **not being merged** until this is live. Inside contributors (elicify.ai Pte. Ltd. employees and direct contractors) are covered by their employment / contractor agreement and do not need to sign separately.

When the CLA is live, the workflow will be:

1. Open a pull request as normal.
2. The `cla-assistant` bot will comment on your PR with a one-click sign link.
3. Click the link, sign in with your GitHub account, fill in your name and email.
4. The bot will mark your CLA as signed and your PR will be unblocked for review.

You only sign once per GitHub account. All future PRs from the same account are automatically covered.

## What if I don't want to sign?

You don't have to. The MIT license lets you fork Omnipus, modify it, and distribute your modified version freely (subject to the [trademark notice](TRADEMARKS.md) on the name and logo). The CLA is only required if you want your changes merged **into the upstream Omnipus repository**.

## Questions

- **Will signing the CLA prevent me from using my own contribution elsewhere?** No. The CLA assigns copyright but grants you a perpetual license back to use your own contribution however you want.
- **What if my employer owns my work?** Have your employer sign the **Corporate CLA**. Until then, contributions on company time can't be accepted.
- **Will Omnipus ever go closed-source?** The upstream MIT code is irrevocable — anyone who has it today keeps the MIT grant forever. The CLA gives us the *option* to distribute the same code under different terms in future commercial variants, while the open-source MIT version continues in parallel.
- **Why not DCO instead?** [DCO](https://developercertificate.org/) is a lighter alternative that asserts contributor authorship without assigning copyright. It works well for projects that commit to staying single-license. Omnipus needs the option to ship commercial variants alongside open source, which DCO doesn't enable — so we chose CLA.
