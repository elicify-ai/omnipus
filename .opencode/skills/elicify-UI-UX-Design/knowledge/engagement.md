# Engagement & Retention Mechanics (lightly flagged)

What keeps users coming back — the toolkit, with each mechanic's ethical
boundary noted inline. These are **dual-use**: the same loop installs flossing or
doomscrolling. The single gate is the **Regret Test** (Eyal): *would a
fully-informed user still choose this and not regret it?* If no, it's a dark
pattern.

## The Hook Model (Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment)

Nir Eyal, *Hooked*. A four-phase loop that turns an external prompt into a
self-triggered habit.
- **Trigger** — external (notification) at first; graduate to *internal* (an
  emotion the user learns to resolve with your product).
- **Action** — the simplest behavior done in anticipation of reward; must be
  frictionless (Fogg B=MAP).
- **Variable reward** — unpredictable payoff (the engine; see below).
- **Investment** — the user puts something in (data, content, config,
  reputation) that loads the next trigger and raises switching cost.

⚠ *Boundary*: crosses the line when the internal trigger exploited is a negative
emotion the product **manufactures or worsens** (FOMO, loneliness), or when
investment is engineered purely as lock-in rather than to make the experience
genuinely better. Engagement ≠ addiction (a compulsive dependency that *harms*
the user).

## Fogg Behavior Model (B=MAP)

Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt, converging at once. Ability is the
durable lever — make the action tiny; place a "hot prompt in the path of a
motivated person."

⚠ *Boundary*: lowering friction is pro-user for things the user wants. It's
manipulative when friction is lowered for the business-favored action (one-click
subscribe) while *added* to the user's path (ten-step cancel — the "Roach Motel",
now FTC-actionable).

## Variable rewards / intermittent reinforcement

Skinner's variable-ratio schedule → highest response rates, most
extinction-resistant. Dopamine encodes reward-*prediction error* (spikes on
*unexpected* reward). Eyal's reward types: Tribe (social), Hunt (resources),
Self (mastery).

Ethical version: reward **variable in delight but reliable in value** — the
variability is in *which* good thing you get, not *whether* you get anything
worthwhile.

⚠ *Boundary* (most abusable mechanic): crosses the line with infinite scroll /
slot-machine refresh engineered for time-on-app, hollow "engagement bait" that
persists after value stops, or chance + money + minors (loot boxes).

## Goal-gradient & endowed progress

Effort rises near a visible goal (Hull 1932; Kivetz et al. 2006). An artificial
head start makes the goal feel closer (Nunes & Drèze 2006: 10-stamp card
pre-stamped twice beats 8-stamp blank). → Progress bars, "2 of 5 done", "80% to
next level", profile-completeness meters.

⚠ *Boundary*: progress must be **honest and completable**. Manipulative when the
bar is fake, the finish line perpetually recedes, or the head start is fictional.

## Streaks

Lifecycle: days 1–7 novelty (white-hat) → days 8–30 pride + something to lose →
day 31+ loss aversion, then identity ("I don't break my streak" — the most
durable, intrinsic state). Duolingo ran 600+ experiments and found making streaks
*easier* raised long-term engagement.

Humane patterns: **streak freezes** ("permission to be human"), **earn-back/
repair** windows, separate the streak from a rigid daily goal, distinct routine
vs urgent notifications. **Design recovery from day one.** Plan to *graduate* the
loss-aversion scaffolding once the habit is intrinsic.

⚠ *Boundary*: trap streaks with no recovery path, manufactured dread, or **selling
streak insurance** to monetize the anxiety you created.

## Investment, IKEA & endowment effects

People over-value what they helped create (Norton/Mochon/Ariely 2012 — but only
when the labor *succeeds*) and what is theirs (endowment). → Let users customize,
curate collections, build reputation; each act raises perceived value and loads
relevant content.

⚠ *Boundary*: investment is white-hat when it makes the product genuinely better
for the user **and is portable/exportable**. It's a dark pattern as a sunk-cost
lock-in / "data ransom" (no export).

## Social mechanics (reciprocity, social proof, relatedness)

Octalysis Core Drive 5. Humans are moved by connection, competition, mentorship,
validation. Honest social proof ("12 colleagues use this"), cooperative goals,
real community.

⚠ *Boundary*: manufactured social pressure ("X people viewing now" when false),
guilt loops (shared streaks punishing both parties), FOMO weaponization,
contact-harvesting ("friend spam").

## The unifying lens

**Octalysis White Hat → Black Hat → White Hat**: open by making the user feel
empowered (white: meaning, accomplishment, creativity), use a *brief* black-hat
moment (scarcity, curiosity, loss) to drive a decision, then **restore the
user's sense of control**. Permanent black-hat = users churn the moment they can
(the Zynga pattern).

**Manipulation Matrix** (Eyal): *Would I use it?* × *Does it improve the user's
life?* → aim for the **Facilitator** quadrant (yes/yes). Avoid the **Dealer**
(no/no).

## Metrics

- Measure **retention depth** (Power User Curve / engagement intensity), not just
  breadth. Optimize the core action that signals *real value*.
- A **flattening retention curve** = value found (the success signal). Treat
  compulsive-but-regretful usage as a red flag, not a win.
- Add **circuit breakers**: "You're all caught up", natural stopping points.

## Dark patterns to avoid (explicit)

Fake urgency/scarcity counters · confirmshaming · roach-motel / hard-to-cancel ·
trap streaks + streak insurance · forced social pressure / friend spam · data
ransom / lock-in · hidden costs · bait-and-switch · pre-ticked opt-ins ·
manufactured incompleteness (unread badges that never zero). Most are now
**EU-illegal** and FTC-actionable.
